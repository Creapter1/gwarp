Terms of Service

Last updated: 9 November 2026

These terms are the rules for using G-Warp: the website, the web editor (app.html), the desktop app and the downloads on this site. They are written in plain language on purpose. By using G-Warp you agree to them. If you don't agree, please don't use G-Warp.

G-Warp is a free, independent beta project made by Creapter. If you are under 13 (or under the age where you live that needs a parent's OK), please read these terms with a parent, guardian or teacher.

1. What G-Warp is

  • A free block-coding editor that works like Scratch, made to open, run and save Scratch-compatible projects (.sb3).
  • G-Warp is not made by, endorsed by or connected to Scratch, the Scratch Foundation, MIT, TurboWarp or PenguinMod. Scratch is a project of the Scratch Foundation; its name and logo are theirs.
  • G-Warp is in beta: features change, and some things may not work yet. Keep your own backups of projects that matter to you.

2. Your projects belong to you

  • You own what you make in G-Warp. We don't claim any rights to your projects, costumes, sounds or songs.
  • G-Warp has no accounts and no project server. Your projects stay on your device (your files and your browser's storage) unless you choose to share them somewhere.
  • Music made with the Song maker and pictures made with G-Warp's tools are yours to use in your projects.
  • When you share a project on another website (for example scratch.mit.edu), that website's own rules and licence apply there. Projects shared on Scratch are under Scratch's licence (CC BY-SA 2.0), so others may remix them.

3. Only use what you have the right to use

  • Only put pictures, sounds, music, code and characters into your projects if you made them, they are free to use, or you have permission.
  • When you remix someone else's project, follow its licence and give credit.
  • If you believe something on this site uses your work without permission, tell us (see Contact) and we will look at it and remove it if needed.

4. Be safe and fair

Don't use G-Warp to:

  • break the law, or to bully, harass, threaten or scam anyone;
  • make or spread harmful software, or to attack, overload or break other services (for example by spamming cloud variables);
  • collect other people's personal information without their permission;
  • pretend to be G-Warp, Scratch or another person or organisation.

Never type passwords, private keys or other secrets into projects, cloud variables or AI prompts.

5. Extensions

  • G-Warp can load extensions made by other people, for example from the TurboWarp and PenguinMod extension galleries. They are their creators' work and keep their own licences.
  • G-Warp labels extensions Safe, Moderate or Dangerous to help you decide. A label is not a guarantee. Read the warning before you add one.
  • Projects that use extensions (or go past Scratch's limits, like songs longer than 3 minutes) may not work on Scratch. G-Warp warns you before that happens.

6. AI helpers

  • G-Warp's AI helpers (like G-Spark and Graffy) can use an AI model that runs on your own computer (for example Ollama or the built-in model in the desktop app). That stays on your computer.
  • If you choose to connect an online AI service with your own key, what you send goes to that service under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • AI can be wrong. Check what it makes before you use it or share it.

7. Online features and privacy

  • Visit counter (website only): the website counts visits and how many people are online right now. It stores only numbers and a random session code: no names, no IP addresses, no location, no cookies. It does not run in downloaded copies or the desktop app.
  • Cloud variables: projects with cloud variables use TurboWarp's cloud server. Only numbers are sent, under a random player name. TurboWarp's rules apply there.
  • Loading projects from the internet: when you open a project or extension from a link, G-Warp downloads it from that site.
  • LAN sharing (desktop app): sharing a project with people on your own network sends it only to the computers that join you.
  • Bug reports (desktop app): when something breaks, G-Warp saves a report in the bug-reports folder next to G-Warp.exe. It includes a list of what you clicked and did in G-Warp since it started, so the bug can be found. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it yourself. Read it first, and remove anything private.
  • See Safety + privacy for more.

  • The G-Warp name, logo and mascots (G-Spark, Grapher, Graffy, Griffy) identify this project. Please don't use them in a way that makes people think your project is the official G-Warp or is endorsed by it.
